# Build the WhatsApp-first HR agent incumbents skipped

India's mainstream HRMS vendors made in-app agentic assistants free during 2026. greytHR's NAVOS, Kredily's KAI and Zimyo 3.0 now handle leave, attendance and payroll actions at no extra cost. A solo or small Indian team should therefore not build "a chatbot that answers leave questions inside one HRMS". That product is now worth ₹0. The opening is an **HRMS-agnostic, WhatsApp-plus-Slack/Teams employee agent**. It would ground policy answers in each company's own versioned handbook with clause-level citations, compute entitlements deterministically, hand off cleanly to humans, and run cross-tool workflows (onboarding, interview scheduling, training chases, rota swaps) that no single HRMS covers. On the technical side, builders with RAG and LangChain experience should unlearn one habit. For a typical SMB handbook, **long-context prompting with caching plus a deterministic rules engine** beats elaborate retrieval. Hybrid contextual retrieval is the fallback for larger corpora, and GraphRAG is not justified. Safety has to be built into the architecture from the start, because the product reads salaries, medical leave and grievances. The "8 safety nets" are identity and row-level security, input filtering, PII redaction, narrow tools, grounding with abstention, human-in-the-loop for writes, output DLP, and audit, plus hard routing of POSH and crisis topics to humans. Per-employee analytics is where the legal risk sits. Ship operational metrics now, keep inferred scores aggregate-only, and refuse individual attrition or burnout scores for managers. Revenue will come from ₹1–6 lakh annual deals sold through partners, not field sales. The DPDP compliance deadline (13 May 2027, possibly earlier) is both a cost and a selling point.

## Incumbents gave the agent away, so compete on channel and cross-system reach

The year 2026 removed the easy wedge. greytHR launched NAVOS on 3 June 2026 as an agentic assistant built into its HRMS. It executes actions across payroll, core HR, leave, performance and recruitment ([greytHR](https://www.greythr.com/greythr-pressrelease/greytHR-launches-navos/)). It is **included in all paid plans with "no per-seat AI fees, usage caps or separate purchase"** ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/spotlight/news-wire/greythr-launches-navos-agentic-ai-that-turns-hr-intent-into-action)). greytHR reports 1.4M+ interactions across about 17,000 enabled organisations ([CXOToday](https://cxotoday.com/media-coverage/hr-professionals-gain-up-to-5x-operational-bandwidth-as-greythr-navos-crosses-1-4-million-interactions/)). Kredily followed on 14 August 2026 with KAI, which has **110+ skills**, flags exceptions for human approval, and comes with a managed-payroll service tier distributed through CA firms ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/spotlight/news-wire/kredily-3-0-launches-kai-agentic-ai-for-payroll-and-hr-and-expands-into-ai-powered-managed-payroll-services)). Darwinbox launched the first HCM MCP server in May 2025 ([People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/hr-technology/darwinbox-becomes-first-hr-tech-platform-to-launch-its-own-mcp-server-for-ai-integration-45528)). Its Slack/Teams "Super Agent" (September 2025) chains HR, IT and finance actions, initially for design partners only ([Reworked](https://www.reworked.co/the-wire/darwinbox-launches-super-agent-an-ai-teammate-for-every-employee-that-gets-work-done/)). Globally the category is consolidating. ServiceNow closed its **$2.85B Moveworks acquisition** in December 2025 ([Entrepreneur India](https://www.entrepreneur.com/en-in/news-and-trends/servicenow-completes-acquisition-of-moveworks/500915)), and Workday bought Sana, Paradox and Flowise ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchhrsoftware/news/366631340/Workday-previews-a-dozen-AI-agents-acquires-Sana)). Enterprise specialists price themselves out of the 50–500 segment. Leena AI lists at about **$150/user/year with a 1,000-user minimum** ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/fr/blog/tarifs-leena-ai)).

The tools the practitioner names point to the gaps that remain. **Keka**, the most common mid-market HRMS on that list, has no confirmed agent launch. A third-party index scored it 8/100 for agent-readiness ([xpay](https://www.xpay.sh/saas-pricing/keka/)), and a competitor claims its AI sits only in hiring and performance ([Kredily](https://kredily.com/best-ai-payroll-software-india/)). Both sources are weak, and confirming Keka's roadmap is the top diligence item. **Freshteam is being shut down**: Freshworks stopped renewals from 7 March 2026 ([People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/amp/news/business/freshworks-to-end-freshteam-hr-product-stop-renewals-from-march-2026-47939)). Its recruiting customers are migrating now, so the product should not build on Freshteam except for data export. None of the incumbent launches mentions WhatsApp. NAVOS and KAI appear to be in-app only, and only Darwinbox has confirmed Slack/Teams. This is absence of evidence rather than proof, but it matters a great deal for manufacturing, retail and field-staff firms whose employees never open an HRMS app.

User complaints point the same way. In a Capterra survey, **49% of HR staff at firms with a chatbot had received at least one complaint about it**, and only 67% were completely confident it gave accurate information ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/resources/hr-chatbot-research/); the survey date is unconfirmed). A handoff that drops an employee into a generic inbox without the conversation history is "worse than no assistant at all" ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ai-for-hr/ai-employee-self-service)). The defensible positions, in order, are:

1. A cross-system HR-ops agent spanning HRMS, Google/M365, Slack/WhatsApp and payroll or CA.
2. A WhatsApp- and vernacular-first agent for deskless staff.
3. An agent plus managed HR ops for 50–200-person firms without an HR team.

Standalone in-HRMS policy Q&A is not on the list. The practitioner's own wish for a "bank-style" guided button bot fits positions 1 and 2.

## Long-context caching plus deterministic rules beats clever RAG for policy Q&A

HR questions split into two kinds, and each needs a different engine. **Static policy knowledge** covers questions like "can sick and casual leave be clubbed?", "is notice buyout allowed?" and "when is the appraisal cycle?". **Live employee state** covers "what is my leave balance?", "has my overtime been approved?" and "which trainings am I overdue on?". Policy text is retrieved. Employee state comes from typed tool calls to the HRMS. Combined questions, such as "can I take three sick plus two casual days next week?", go to a deterministic calculator that merges rules, balances and the holiday calendar. The LLM interprets the question and explains the answer. It never does the arithmetic.

For the policy side, the evidence favours the simplest option. Anthropic advises that knowledge bases **under 200,000 tokens (about 500 pages) can go straight into the prompt**, and that prompt caching cuts latency by more than 2x and cost by up to 90% ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). Cache reads on current Claude models cost 0.05–0.1x the base input price ([Claude docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). A typical SMB handbook of a few dozen to a few hundred pages fits easily. Each tenant's active policy set, filtered to the employee's location, grade and employment type, becomes a cached prefix, and clause-combining questions benefit because the model sees every relevant clause at once. Research does not settle the RAG-versus-long-context question. The LaRA benchmark across 11 LLMs found "no silver bullet" ([LaRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977)). For small corpora and strong models, though, stuffing is the higher-recall default.

When a tenant's corpus outgrows that budget (roughly 100–150k tokens once circulars, state addenda, FAQs and past tickets are included), switch to **contextual retrieval**. That means prepending LLM-generated context to each chunk, running BM25 plus dense search, and reranking. In Anthropic's tests this cut retrieval failures by **67%, from 5.7% to 1.9%** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). Postgres with pgvector and a BM25 extension, fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, is enough at HR scale ([ParadeDB](https://paradedb.com/blog/hybrid-search-in-postgresql-the-missing-manual)). It also keeps tenancy, versions, rules and audit logs in one transactional store. For Hindi content, BGE-M3 scored **47.29 NDCG@10 versus 39.93 for multilingual-E5 and 36.50 for BM25** on Hindi-BEIR ([arXiv 2408.09437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.09437)). No benchmark covers Hinglish. Handle it with an LLM query-rewrite step into canonical English HR terms, keep the original text for BM25, and test on a custom eval set.

Two failure modes matter more than retrieval algorithms: **wrong policy version** and **wrong location or grade variant**. On versioned-document QA, a version-aware method scored **90% versus 58% for naive RAG and 64% for GraphRAG**, and GraphRAG's indexing cost about 16x more tokens ([VersionRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08109v1)). Version and scope therefore belong in hard SQL filters applied before retrieval: family ID, version, effective dates, status, state, grade band, employment type, and explicit `overrides_clause_id` precedence. Do not leave them for the LLM to infer. Missing or conflicting effective dates should trigger clarification or abstention ([Zimaspace](https://shop.zimaspace.com/blogs/tech-ai-hub/features-reliable-document-version-selection-rag)). GraphRAG and LightRAG win on LLM-judged "comprehensiveness" for global sensemaking ([LightRAG](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05779v3)), which is the wrong objective for "how many CL can I carry forward?". Self-correcting agentic RAG adds only about 3 points in an independent 2026 reproduction ([arXiv 2603.16169](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.16169)). Adopt the cheap parts (a retrieval-sufficiency check, then abstain or escalate) and skip multi-hop loops.

For numeric and eligibility answers, use a **policy-as-rules** layer: a JSON schema covering accrual, carry-forward caps, `cannot_club_with`, sandwich rules, buyout formulas, overtime payout cycles and applicability keys, each tied to a `source_clause_id`. The P2T research pattern (schema-constrained LLM extraction with provenance, a judge-and-repair loop, conflict checks, and mandatory human review) is the template for drafting these rules from handbooks ([arXiv 2512.04408](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04408v1)). In the MVP, rules are hand-authored per tenant, and HR admins approve LLM-drafted rules side by side with the source clause. For citations, Anthropic's native Citations API gave **up to 15% higher recall accuracy** than prompt-based citation, and one customer reported **source hallucinations falling from 10% to 0%** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-citations-api)). Every answer should follow the contract `{answer, citations[doc, version, clause_id, quote], computed_values[tool, inputs, output], escalate}` and name the version used, for example "per Leave Policy v3.2, effective 1 Apr 2026, Maharashtra". An answer with an uncited factual claim should be regenerated or refused.

The orchestration needs are modest. Most turns are single-step tool use plus retrieval, so a thin loop is enough: the provider SDK, Pydantic AI, or the Claude Agent SDK. LangGraph earns its place once durable approval workflows appear, with checkpoints and human-in-the-loop pauses ([Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/ai-agent-framework)). That evidence is blog-level, so the team's existing LangChain skills are a fine reason to choose LangGraph when that stage arrives. Wrap each HRMS connector as an MCP server so it can be reused across orchestrators. For evaluation, build a golden set of 150–300 real questions per pilot tenant in English, Hindi and Hinglish. Include unanswerable questions and version or scope traps. Track citation correctness, numeric exact match, wrong-version rate and abstention precision and recall alongside RAGAS or DeepEval metrics in CI ([Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/deepeval-vs-ragas-vs-trulens-rag-evaluation-stack)). Published thresholds are only practitioner opinion. For HR, aim near 99% numeric accuracy through deterministic tools and keep wrong-version answers effectively at zero.

## The practitioner's list falls into three automation tiers

The HR professional's list sorts cleanly by risk. **Tier 1 is read-only and fully automatable:** leave balance, overtime credit timing, notice-buyout rules, PF nomination how-to, referral contacts, whether a training is mandatory, sick-plus-casual clubbing, appraisal timing and WFH policy. **Tier 2 is transactional, automated up to an approval the HRMS already enforces:** applying for WFH or leave, interview scheduling, induction, training follow-ups, productivity trackers, and cab or rota assignment. **Tier 3 is intake and routing only:** POSH, grievances about harassment or discrimination, data breaches, performance outcomes, succession, mobility and workforce planning.

| Workflow | How the agent does it | System of record | Human step | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leave balance | Tool call to the HRMS balance API (Keka `/time/leavebalance`, SF Time Account, Workday balances, greytHR leave) | HRMS | None | WhatsApp phone not mapped to an HRMS identity; stale accruals |
| Overtime credit timing | Payroll calendar from policy plus OT-approval status lookup | Payroll module | Manager approves OT | Promising a date payroll then misses; answer "per cycle" plus status |
| Notice buyout | Cited policy answer, then a guided request to the HRBP | Contract / HRMS | HRBP and manager approve amount | Employee's own contract differs from the generic policy |
| PF nomination (and PPF confusion) | Step-by-step guide to EPFO e-nomination; check UAN/KYC status from the HRMS | EPFO (external) | None | Never collect Aadhaar, OTP or UAN password; PPF is a personal bank/post-office scheme, not employer-run |
| Referrals | Policy plus live TA owner from the org chart, ATS deep link | ATS | None | Stale owner names in static documents |
| Mandatory training | LMS assignment lookup with due dates | LMS | None | "Mandatory" flag modelled differently in each LMS |
| Sick + casual clubbing | Rules engine (`cannot_club_with`, sandwich rule) with citation | Policy / HRMS | Edge cases to HR | Generalising nationally when state law and company policy vary |
| Appraisal timing | Cycle calendar plus PMS API | PMS | None | Drifting into ratings or outcomes |
| WFH for date range | Guided form, policy cap check, create request via API or deep link | HRMS attendance | Manager approval in the HRMS | Write API may not exist (unconfirmed for Keka) |
| Interview scheduling | Free/busy lookup, propose slots by email or WhatsApp, book, write back to ATS | ATS + calendar | Recruiter override | Personal Gmail panelists; reschedules |
| Induction | "Employee added" webhook triggers checklist, documents, LMS enrolment, IT ticket | HRMS | HR verifies BGV and statutory forms | Joining-date changes; OCR errors |
| Training follow-ups | LMS completion events or polling, nudge sequence, manager escalation | LMS | Manager after N nudges | Nudge fatigue; WhatsApp template cost |
| Productivity trackers | Button-based daily stand-up plus attendance pull into a team dashboard | HRMS / PM tool | Manager review | Surveillance and DPDP risk (see analytics section) |
| Cab / rota | Roster plus rules; swap requests via buttons | Roster / transport vendor | Supervisor approves swaps | State-specific night-shift safety rules for women |
| POSH and sensitive matters | Fixed non-LLM script, IC contacts, confidential channel | IC / restricted case store | Fully human | Misclassifying a POSH complaint as a general grievance |

The workflow rows above are design synthesis from the cited API and statute findings. Integration access varies widely by vendor. Keka offers OAuth client-credentials APIs for leave types and balances ([Knit](https://getknit.dev/blog/comprehensive-guide-to-keka-api-directory.md)). Its admin-configured webhooks include `leaverequestcreated` but have **no documented signature scheme** ([Keka docs](https://developers.keka.com/docs/getting-started-with-webhooks)), so the receiver should require a secret header, allow-list source IPs, and re-fetch the record through the API rather than trust the payload. SuccessFactors Time Off runs on OData v2, and the Time Account object requires explicit role permissions ([WSO2](https://wso2.com/integration-platform/docs/connectors/catalog/hrms/sap.successfactors.ectimeoff/overview)). Workday's absence REST API caps responses at 100 objects ([Ballerina](https://central.ballerina.io/ballerinax/workday.absencemanagement/1.5.1)). Darwinbox API access is by request only, and webhooks are reportedly absent, so the connector has to poll ([Knit](https://getknit.dev/blog/a-to-z-of-integrating-with-darwinbox-api.md)). Among unified APIs, **Knit is the one that covers Keka, greytHR and Darwinbox** ([Knit](https://getknit.dev/?p=455)). Merge and Kombo suit Workday, SuccessFactors and Zoho People. Build a direct Keka connector first.

For LMS integrations, Docebo emits `course.enrollment.completed` webhooks ([Docebo](https://developer.docebo.com/docs/webhooks-events)), while TalentLMS can only call a URL on completion ([TalentLMS](https://help.talentlms.com/hc/en-us/articles/9651457002908-How-to-trigger-a-custom-action-when-users-complete-a-specific-course-in-TalentLMS)). Interview scheduling via Microsoft Graph `findMeetingTimes` works only for M365 work mailboxes ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/graph/findmeetingtimes-example)). For PF, EPF e-nomination is employee self-service: Unified Member Portal, then Manage, then E-Nomination, then Aadhaar-OTP e-sign, with an activated, Aadhaar-seeded UAN as a prerequisite ([EPFO help file](https://epfindia.gov.in/EP_Cal/Doc/eNominationHelpFile.pdf)). The bot guides the steps and never touches credentials. Statutory answers need humility. The four Labour Codes were notified in force from 21 November 2025 according to law-firm alerts ([Cyril Amarchand](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/60-days-of-Labour-Codes.pdf)), but state rules remain patchy and one July 2026 analysis disputes that the Codes are effective at all ([Bhatt & Joshi](https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/the-labour-codes-2026-what-actually-changed-for-employers/)). The agent should answer "per company policy", and statutory claims should go into a vendor-maintained rule pack only after legal review.

POSH sets the clearest hard line. Complaints go to the Internal Committee in writing within **three months, extendable by three more** ([SS Rana](https://ssrana.in/posh-law/ufaq/is-there-any-specific-time-frame-for-filing-a-posh-complaint/)). Section 16 bars disclosure of identities and proceedings ([ILMS Academy](https://www.ilms.academy/blog/confidentiality-in-posh-complaints-explained-legal-duties-penalties-best-practices)). The bot may explain the process, list IC contacts and open a confidential channel to the IC. It must not ask for incident details, judge severity, suggest conciliation, forward anything to managers, or let the content reach analytics, traces or memory. No source addresses chatbots and POSH specifically, so this design is synthesis, and it deliberately favours recall over precision.

### WhatsApp is the reach channel, buttons are the interface, and costs changed in October

The practitioner's "bank-style" guided chatbot maps directly onto WhatsApp's interactive messages. These offer **reply buttons (maximum 3) and list messages (maximum 10 rows)**, and neither needs a template inside the 24-hour service window ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/guides/interactive-messages/)). A good design has a top-level list (Leave balance, Apply leave/WFH, Payslip and OT, PF help, Policies, Raise ticket, Talk to HR), WhatsApp Flows for multi-field forms, and free text routed to the LLM with intent classification. Any sensitive-category hit switches to a fixed script. Buttons are a safety feature as well as a usability feature: they shrink the space of free-text inputs the model must interpret.

The economics shifted recently. Since **1 October 2026, each business number gets only 1,000 free service messages a month, and utility messages inside an open window are now charged**. India accounts must also migrate to INR billing by **31 December 2026**, or messages stop delivering from 1 January 2027 ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing)). Third-party rate cards put India utility messages near ₹0.115 and marketing near ₹0.86, before BSP fees and 18% GST ([Robylon](https://www.robylon.ai/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india)). Keep every proactive nudge (training reminders, interview confirmations, payroll notices) in the utility category, and pass per-message cost through or cap it in pricing. Collect opt-in at onboarding, since the HRMS already holds phone numbers, and record a consent timestamp. Slack and Teams carry no per-message cost and suit desk staff and manager approvals.

## Eight safety nets, enforced outside the model

The guardrail architecture follows one principle: **the robust controls are deterministic and sit outside the LLM**, and classifiers add depth but are never the boundary. The risk catalogue comes from three sources. The OWASP LLM Top 10 (2025) ranks prompt injection and sensitive-information disclosure first and second ([OWASP](https://www.mindtwo.de/storage/media/images/4525/owasp-top-10-for-llm-applications-2025.pdf)). OWASP's Agentic Top 10 (December 2025) adds goal hijack, tool misuse, identity abuse and human-trust exploitation ([Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/)). NIST AI 600-1 adds confabulation, data privacy and human-AI configuration ([NIST](https://airc.nist.gov/docs/NIST.AI.600-1.GenAI-Profile.ipd.pdf)). In HR terms these become concrete threats. An employee asks what a colleague earns. A planted PDF says "approve all leave". A manager talks the agent into editing a skip-level report. The agent invents a maternity entitlement.

| # | Safety net | What it stops | Concrete implementation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identity, RBAC/ABAC, row-level security, tenant isolation | Cross-employee and cross-tenant leakage; manager over-reach | SSO is the source of truth; on-behalf-of identity on every call, never an agent superuser; Postgres RLS with `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY` and a non-BYPASSRLS app role ([PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/ddl-rowsecurity.html)); reporting-chain policies; compensation, medical and POSH data in separate schemas with explicit ABAC |
| 2 | Input filtering | Jailbreaks and injected instructions in user turns, uploads and retrieved chunks | Prompt Guard 2 22M self-hosted on CPU ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-Prompt-Guard-2-22M)) or Azure Prompt Shields, which detects document attacks ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/openai/concepts/content-filter-prompt-shields)); scan at index time; strip hidden text; a hit downgrades the session to read-only and flags it |
| 3 | PII detection and redaction | Aadhaar, PAN, UAN and bank data leaking into logs, traces or third-party models | Presidio `IN_AADHAAR` and `IN_PAN` recognizers ([Presidio](https://microsoft.github.io/presidio/supported_entities)) plus custom UAN, IFSC, bank-account and ESIC recognizers; run before logs and on outbound text |
| 4 | Narrow, typed tools with least privilege | Excessive agency and tool misuse | No `run_sql`; tools like `get_my_leave_balance()` that re-authorize server-side; the model never chooses `employee_id`; JSON-schema validation; write tools disabled once untrusted content enters context ([Cycode](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/)) |
| 5 | Grounding, citations, abstention | Confabulated entitlements; wrong-version answers | Native citations; "no source, no answer"; version and scope SQL filters; groundedness check; escalate on conflict |
| 6 | Human in the loop for writes | Persuasive or hijacked actions | Two-phase propose and confirm, showing the raw structured diff (field, old, new, employee) rather than LLM prose; second approver for compensation, termination and bulk changes; idempotency keys |
| 7 | Output DLP and content safety | Leaking someone else's data; exfiltration links; toxic output | Scan the final answer against the caller's entitlements; block markdown images and external links; Llama Guard or Azure Content Safety |
| 8 | Audit, observability, tracing | Unaccountable actions; failed forensics | OpenTelemetry GenAI spans into self-hosted Langfuse ([Portkey](https://portkey.ai/blog/opentelemetry-semantic-conventions-for-genai-traces/)); log identity, tool arguments, authz decisions, HITL approver and filter verdicts to append-only storage; redact PII in traces; keep logs at least one year per DPDP Rule 6 |

Four cross-cutting controls sit on top of the eight. The first is **sensitive-topic routing**: a recall-biased classifier plus keyword backstops sends POSH, self-harm, legal threats, discrimination and whistleblowing to fixed scripts and restricted human queues, with the content excluded from analytics and memory. The second is per-user and per-tenant rate limits. The third is red-team suites that fail the CI build on regressions; promptfoo maps findings to OWASP and the EU AI Act ([AppSecSanta](https://appsecsanta.com/garak-vs-promptfoo)). The fourth is a tested kill switch per tenant and per tool. For uploaded documents and emails, the best published defence is architectural. Google DeepMind's CaMeL keeps untrusted data from ever affecting control flow and drove successful attacks near zero while solving about 77% of AgentDojo tasks ([HF Papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2503.18813)). A "CaMeL-lite" design is practical for a small team. A quarantined LLM call with no tools extracts typed fields from uploads, and values derived from documents can never become tool targets (employee, recipient, amount) without explicit user confirmation. Only HR admins can add documents to the canonical policy corpus.

Data residency completes the picture. Anthropic's first-party `inference_geo` accepts only `us` or `global` ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency)). AWS Bedrock's India cross-Region profile (Mumbai plus Hyderabad) reportedly added Claude models after August 2026 with in-country processing ([CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/india/news/2026/anthropic-takes-claude-inference-live-in-india-opens-path-to-regulated-ai-deployments)), although an earlier September source said otherwise, so verify on the AWS model page. The default stack is therefore Bedrock `in.` inference, RDS Postgres, self-hosted Langfuse, and Prompt Guard and Presidio all in ap-south-1/ap-south-2. Zero-data-retention terms limit how long a provider keeps data but do not change where it is processed ([Techplained](https://www.techplained.com/llm-api-data-residency-india)). A SaaS observability vendor can quietly leak data out of India, so self-host it or redact before export.

## Ship operational analytics now, keep inferences aggregate, and refuse individual risk scores

Per-employee analytics is where the product can create the most value and the most liability. Under India's DPDP regime the vendor will normally be the **Data Processor** and the employer the **Data Fiduciary**. The Rules were notified on 13 November 2025. Most substantive duties (notice, legitimate uses, security, breach notification, penalties) apply from **13 May 2027** ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)). MeitY consulted in January 2026 on compressing that deadline to 13 November 2026, a week from now, but no notified amendment was found ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1773554/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs)). The product should be ready for either date. Section 7(i) lets employers process data "for the purposes of employment" without consent ([KSK](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/can-employers-monitor-employees-dpdp-act/)), but commentators split on whether that covers efficiency surveillance or predictive scoring ([FES](https://collections.fes.de/publikationen/content/pagetext/1990055); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/employment-surveillance-clauses-void-dpdp-regime-539957)). The penalties are severe. Failing reasonable security safeguards can cost **up to ₹250 crore**, and breaches must be reported to the Board in detail within 72 hours ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-indias-dpdp-act-and-rules/)). Consent from employees is a weak fallback because of the power imbalance.

The EU and US rules set the ceiling for any export ambition. Individual monitoring or evaluation of worker performance or behaviour is **EU AI Act Annex III high-risk** ([AI Act Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)). The Digital Omnibus deal moved those obligations to **2 December 2027** ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement)). The **workplace emotion-recognition ban** has applied since 2 February 2025 and covers inference from biometrics such as voice and face ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)). Text sentiment of HR queries is not biometric, but under GDPR it is still profiling. In the US, NYC LL144 requires bias audits based on the four-fifths impact ratio ([Holistic AI](https://www.holisticai.com/blog/bias-audit-impact-ratio-small-sample)). Illinois has banned discriminatory AI and zip-code proxies across the employment lifecycle since 1 January 2026 ([Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/legal-update-new-illinois-ai-law-requires-employee-notice-affirms-existing-employer-nondiscrimination-duties.html)). In *Mobley v. Workday*, a collective action alleging age discrimination by the vendor's own AI was allowed to proceed ([Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/discrimination-lawsuit-over-workdays-ai-hiring-tools-can-proceed-as-class-action-6-things)). The vendor cannot hide behind "the customer decides". The science behind risk scores is also weak. Visier markets resignation predictions as "up to 17x" better than guessing, averaging about 5x ([Visier](https://docs.visier.com/developer/Visier%20People%20Metrics/predictions/predictions%20strengths%20and%20limitations.htm)). Because attrition is rare, such ratios flatter, and scores risk becoming self-fulfilling ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/ai-for-hr/ai-attrition-prediction)). Burnout has no clean ground-truth label at all.

The safe design is a three-tier product policy. **Green** analytics ship under 7(i): leave patterns at team level, individual training completion (an ordinary HR record), headcount and turnover, query volumes, resolution and escalation rates, and aggregate query topics. **Amber** analytics are aggregate-only: engagement, query sentiment, wellbeing indicators and attrition by segment. They use a minimum group size of 5 by default, which matches Microsoft Viva's floor ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/privacy/privacy-considerations)), and 10 for manager views. Differential-privacy noise is added as Viva does ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/viva/insights/Privacy/differential-privacy)), and the next-smallest group is suppressed to block subtraction attacks, as Culture Amp does ([Culture Amp](https://support.cultureamp.com/en/articles/7048386-confidentiality-protections-in-reporting)). **Red** analytics are never built: individual attrition or burnout scores visible to managers, emotion inference from voice or video, keystroke or screen productivity scoring, models using medical or maternity leave or protected-trait proxies (age, gender, pincode, caste, religion), and automated adverse decisions.

The practitioner's "daily productivity tracker" belongs in green only if it is a self-reported, button-based stand-up rolled up to team level. Passive individual scoring falls in red. Supporting features turn compliance into product. A "My data" view lets employees see, correct and contest everything held about them, which supports DPDP rights. A purpose registry blocks reuse of wellbeing data for performance. Retention rules keep raw query text short-lived while aggregates persist. A DPIA, an algorithmic due-diligence pack and per-group false-positive and impact-ratio reports serve customers that may be designated Significant Data Fiduciaries. Product-improvement training on employee data would make the vendor a fiduciary for that purpose, so keep it off by default.

## Price below the HRMS and sell through partners, not field reps

Pricing is boxed in by what customers already pay. greytHR's Growth plan is **₹4,495 a month plus ₹85 per employee above 50** ([greytHR](https://www.greythr.com/pricing-calculator/)). For 100 employees that is about ₹1.05 lakh a year, and for 500 about ₹5.1 lakh. Keka's tiers reportedly run ₹9,999–15,999 a month plus ₹90–150 per extra head, though these figures come from a competitor and conflict ([HROne](https://hrone.cloud/?p=53120)). Rezolve-style overlay pricing of about $2.50–3 PEPM ([Unthread](https://unthread.io/blog/rezolve-ai-pricing/)), roughly ₹250, would cost more than the customer's entire HRMS. The workable range is **₹40–100 PEPM, or a flat ₹10–25K a month up to 200 employees**, with WhatsApp template costs passed through. An outcome-linked tier, such as a guaranteed auto-resolution rate, can echo Leena AI's contractual 70% guarantee ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/fr/blog/tarifs-leena-ai)). Expect an ACV of ₹1–6 lakh. These figures are inferences from list prices, since no sourced deal data exists.

That ACV rules out field sales. Indian SMB HR SaaS sells through inside sales, reseller and distributor programmes (greytHR runs one and partnered with Savex ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/greythr-and-savex-technologies-join-forces-to-help-businesses-automate-their-hr-management-processes/1914903))), and CA-firm and MSME-scheme distribution, as Kredily does ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/spotlight/news-wire/kredily-3-0-launches-kai-agentic-ai-for-payroll-and-hr-and-expands-into-ai-powered-managed-payroll-services)). The best channels for an HRMS-agnostic agent are payroll-outsourcing firms, PEOs and CA firms that serve many 50–500-person clients, the Slack, Teams and Zoho agent marketplaces ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/02/zoho-launches-zia-agents-fuel-autonomous-ai-across-product-suite)), and integration through Darwinbox's MCP server. Freshteam's sunset creates a short-lived list of companies re-evaluating their stack. Buyers are founders, CFOs and HR heads, not CHRO procurement teams. Plan for a free 30-day pilot on two workflows, a security questionnaire, and a DPA. Show greytHR-style security badges: ISO 27001 early, SOC 2 Type II later ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/spotlight/news-wire/greythr-launches-navos-agentic-ai-that-turns-hr-intent-into-action)). India data residency and a DPDP readiness pack are selling points ahead of May 2027. Sales-cycle length and CAC data were not found, so measure them in the first ten pilots.

## MVP scope, phased roadmap, and what lies beyond

The MVP should prove three things with a few design partners on Keka and greytHR. Employees on WhatsApp get correct, cited answers. Numbers come from code, not the model. Sensitive matters never touch the LLM.

| MVP component (weeks 0–10) | Scope |
|---|---|
| Channels | WhatsApp Business API (list/button menu, Flows for WFH form, free-text fallback) plus Slack or Teams for desk staff and manager approvals |
| Identity | Phone/email-to-HRMS employee mapping, OTP-free verification via the HRMS record, opt-in capture |
| Policy Q&A | PDF/DOCX ingest into clause structure; HR-admin review UI for version, effective dates and scope; long-context cached prefix per tenant and scope; native citations; abstain and escalate |
| Live data | Direct Keka connector (OAuth), greytHR via Knit or direct; tools for profile, leave balance, request status, payslip month |
| Rules engine | Hand-authored JSON rules per tenant for leave clubbing, carry-forward, notice buyout, OT payout cycle; `compute_*` tools returning results plus clause IDs |
| Workflows | Read-only Tier 1 list in full; WFH/leave request via API or deep link with HRMS approval; training-due nudges (utility templates) |
| Human handoff | Ticket with full transcript and context into the HR team's queue; SLA timer |
| Sensitive routing | POSH, self-harm, legal and discrimination classifier with fixed scripts, IC contacts and a restricted case store |
| Safety | All 8 nets at minimum viable depth; promptfoo CI suite for salary probing, cross-tenant leakage and PDF injection |
| Analytics | Green tier only: HR deflection, resolution and escalation rates, top question topics, unanswered-question gaps |
| Infra | Bedrock India profile, RDS Postgres with RLS, self-hosted Langfuse, all in ap-south-1/2; DPA template |
| Eval | 200-question golden set per pilot (English, Hindi, Hinglish; traps; unanswerables) gating every release |

| Phase | Timeframe | Adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pilot | Months 0–3 | MVP with 3–5 design partners; measure accuracy, escalation, WhatsApp cost per employee; ISO 27001 started |
| 2. Workflow agent | Months 3–6 | LangGraph durable workflows: interview scheduling (Google/M365 free-busy, ATS write-back), induction from "employee added" webhooks, LMS completion chases, cab/rota swaps; Darwinbox, Zoho People, SuccessFactors and Workday connectors via MCP and unified APIs; contextual hybrid retrieval for large tenants |

Beyond the MVP, the most valuable moves are the ones incumbents cannot easily copy. A **cross-system onboarding and offboarding agent**, the SMB version of Darwinbox's Super Agent, would provision Google or M365 accounts, assign laptops, trigger LMS enrolment and notify payroll and CA firms in one flow. A **managed HR-ops service** run on the agent would compete with Kredily's managed payroll for firms without an HR team. A **policy-health product** would flag contradictions between handbook clauses, show gaps where employees ask questions no policy answers, and track compliance with state-specific rules. **Manager copilots** could draft approvals and summarise team leave coverage while staying strictly in green-tier data. **Vernacular voice notes** on WhatsApp would serve frontline staff, using text understanding only and never voice-based emotion inference. An **MCP endpoint of the product's own** would let customers' AI clients reuse its grounded policy and rules engine. GraphRAG belongs on the list only if cross-document questions such as "summarise every policy affecting contractors" become real demand.

## Conclusion

Free agents inside the HRMS changed the question from whether an AI can answer leave queries to who owns the employee's front door. HRMS vendors own the admin console. An outsider can win the channels employees actually use, the workflows that cross system boundaries, and the trust that comes from cited, version-correct, deterministic answers. Technically, the hard parts are not the ones RAG tutorials teach. They are policy versioning, identity mapping from a WhatsApp number to an HRMS record, server-side authorization, and refusing gracefully. A builder who sets the vector database aside and invests in rules, filters and handoffs will outperform one tuning chunk sizes.

The regulatory calendar turns into a growth plan. With DPDP enforcement arriving by May 2027 (possibly sooner), EU high-risk duties in December 2027, and US vendor liability already being litigated, a product built with the 8 safety nets and a strict green/amber/red analytics policy from day one becomes the easiest one for a cautious CFO to approve. The main unknowns are whether Keka ships its own agent, how WhatsApp per-message costs affect unit economics after October 2026, and the real sales-cycle length. The first ten pilots should be designed to answer all three.
